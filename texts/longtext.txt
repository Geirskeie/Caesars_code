The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the cliff for more than a hundred years. Every night the keeper climbed the narrow stairs, lit the great lamp and watched the ships pass far out at sea. Most of them never knew his name, but many of them owed him their lives.

When the storms came in from the north, the waves rose higher than the houses in the village below. The keeper would stay awake through the whole night, making sure the light never went out. In the morning he would walk down to the harbour, drink a cup of strong coffee and listen to the fishermen talk about the weather.

Years later the lighthouse was replaced by a small automatic beacon. The keeper moved into a house by the water, where he spent his days repairing old boats and teaching the children how to read the sky. He often said that the sea had taught him patience, and that patience was the only thing a man really needed to learn.

Today the tower is a museum. Visitors climb the same stairs, look out over the same water and try to imagine what it was like to keep a light burning through the darkest hours of the year.
